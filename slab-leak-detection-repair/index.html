Symptom-first.

Why is my floor warm, damp or my bill higher?

Water under a slab does not always show up as a puddle. Instead, a hot water line leaking beneath the floor may warm a patch of tile or carpet. A cold line leaking can leave a damp spot, a musty smell or a crack in a baseboard. The most common sign is a water bill that rises with no change in habits.

These are all clues pointing toward what plumbers call a slab leak, and the fix begins with locating it precisely before anyone cuts concrete.

Emergency service.

Emergency slab leak repair in West Chester: when to call tonight.

Warm spots on a floor, the sound of running water with every fixture off, or a meter that spins when the house is quiet can mean a pipe is leaking under the concrete. Water under a slab damages flooring and the foundation edge, so these calls do not need to wait for business hours.

Pinpointing the leak comes first. Tracing equipment and listening devices locate the break before any concrete is opened, so the plumber breaks only what is necessary.

What to do until the emergency plumber arrives.

1

Shut off the main water valve.

This stops the leak while you wait. Run a faucet to confirm the meter stops.

2

Check for warm floor spots.

Walk barefoot over tile or wood. Warm patches often sit above a hot water line leak.

3

Move belongings off the floor.

Lift rugs and furniture legs off damp areas and call your insurance carrier if flooring is wet.

Signs of a leak under a concrete floor.

  • The water meter keeps moving after every fixture is off.
  • A warm spot appears on a tile, laminate or carpeted floor.
  • You hear running water when nothing is on.
  • Baseboards swell, flooring lifts or grout cracks near an interior wall.
  • A damp patch or musty smell appears with no visible source.
  • Water pressure drops across the house without a known cause.

Spot repair or reroute.

Spot repair fits when.

  • One short section of pipe has failed.
  • The rest of the line tests sound.
  • The floor above is easy to open.

A reroute fits when.

  • Leaks keep appearing in the same line.
  • The line runs under finished flooring or tile.
  • The pipe material is old or thin-walled.

Rerouting means running a new line through walls or the attic instead of beneath the slab. It costs more at first, and it avoids opening concrete again.

Slab leaks around West Chester.

Slab leaks are a concern wherever homes sit on concrete rather than over a basement. Many houses in the area have basements, so a damp patch is more often a pipe leak behind a wall or a groundwater issue. If you are unsure, start with leak detection visits, which covers walls, ceilings and slabs.

If the leak is on the water service line between the meter and the house, see service line leak repair. A rising bill without a visible leak is covered in a high water bill check.

What to do if you suspect a leak under the floor.

Shut off the main if a floor feels hot, because a hot water line can scald, and keep bare feet off warm spots. Pressure testing for a slab leak is done according to the test pressures the plumbing code and the pipe manufacturer specify, and those standards differ by pipe material.

Additions and garages in Westtown and elsewhere can sit on a slab even when the main house has a basement. If a floor in one of those feels warm, mention it when you call.

A slab leak is a leak in a line that runs under a concrete floor, which is more common in postwar houses than in the 1800s buildings downtown. A warm spot on the floor or a meter that moves with everything off is the usual first sign, and it is worth mentioning if your house sits on a slab.

Signs a slab leak is behind your bill or your floor.

A hot water line under the slab can warm the floor, while a cold line leak may only show as higher bills and damp carpet. Cracks in tile and the sound of running water at night are other clues.

Repair options include rerouting the line through walls or attic, or breaking a section of slab and repairing the pipe. The plumber explains both with a price before choosing.
