What to do if you suspect a frozen pipe.

1

Open the faucet.

Leave the affected tap open so melting water and steam have a place to go.

2

Find the cold spot.

Frost on a pipe, a section cold to the touch or a wall that is cold on the outside are clues.

3

Warm it gently.

Use a hair dryer on low heat, warm towels or a space heater at a safe distance, starting nearest the faucet.

4

Check for leaks.

Once water flows, look along the pipe for drips. If you see one, shut off the main and call.

Why pipes split where they do.

Water expands about nine percent when it freezes. Ice forms first in the coldest part of a pipe and grows toward the warmer end, trapping liquid water between the ice and a closed faucet. That trapped section has nowhere to go, so pressure climbs until the pipe wall gives way, often at a seam or a fitting rather than where the ice formed.

This is why a pipe can seem fine while frozen and flood the house once it thaws. The split is already there, held shut by the ice.

West Chester winters and what that means for pipes.

Overnight temperatures in southeastern Pennsylvania drop into the teens during cold snaps, and pipes in unheated spaces are first to freeze. A faucet that slows to a trickle on a cold morning is a sign of ice partway down the line.

If you are leaving the house during a freeze, keep the heat at a minimum safe temperature, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and know where your main shutoff is.

If the pipe has already split.

Shut off the main water valve and see burst pipe repair for the next steps. Wet walls and floors fall under more on this topic. For a house with a cold zone or heating trouble, boiler repair may be part of the answer.

A cold-night checklist.

  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room heat reaches the pipes.
  • Let one faucet on the coldest side of the house drip slightly.
  • Disconnect outdoor hoses and close the interior valve to any exterior spigot.
  • Keep the thermostat at a minimum safe temperature even when away.
  • Know where the main shutoff is and make sure everyone in the house can find it.

A frozen pipe discovered before a split is a warning to fix the cause, not only the symptom. Insulating the pipe or sealing a draft is cheaper than repeating a thaw every cold snap.

What to do before you thaw a pipe.

Open the tap, then warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer, never a torch. Follow the heat cable manufacturer's guidelines if you use one, and shut off the main at the first sign of a split.

Garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls in Westtown are the places pipes freeze first in a cold snap, and the township page includes a seasonal checklist. Check it before the first hard freeze.

Pipes in exterior walls and unheated rooms of older Gay Street rowhouses freeze first on the coldest nights. Open a tap slightly and keep the room warm, and shut the main if a split shows when the ice melts.
