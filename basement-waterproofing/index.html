Basement Waterproofing in West Chester, PA.

Water in a basement comes from one of three places: the ground, the roof, or a leaking pipe. Waterproofing only works when it targets the right one.

Diagnosis.

Is the water groundwater, roof runoff or a pipe?

A wet basement has three common sources. Groundwater pushes through the floor-wall joint or cracks after long rain. Roof and surface water drains toward the foundation when gutters overflow or the lot slopes toward the house. And a leaking pipe or fixture can leave puddles that look exactly like seepage.

A simple test helps. Tape a square of plastic wrap to the wall. If moisture forms under it, humidity is condensing.

If it forms on the wall side, water is coming through. If the wall is dry but a puddle appears near a pipe, the source is plumbing.

Start with the outside before the inside.

Before paying for a basement system, check the cheap fixes that often matter most. Gutters should be clear, and downspouts should discharge several feet from the foundation. The soil should slope away from the house for the first few feet. Window wells should be clean and drain.

Plenty of damp basements improve after these steps alone. They also reduce the work an interior system has to do later, and they are worth doing even if you do install a drain and sump pump.

Interior drainage or exterior work.

Interior drainage fits when.

  • Water appears at the floor-wall joint.
  • The yard has plantings or paving and is hard to dig.
  • You want one system that keeps water off the floor.

Exterior work fits when.

  • Walls are seeping from the outside.
  • A cracked or missing membrane is the cause.
  • Access around the house allows excavation.

Exterior excavation in Pennsylvania requires a call to 811 at least three business days ahead, and the contractor must file their own ticket.

The September 2021 storm and why it matters here.

The remnants of Hurricane Ida put 6 to 8 inches of rain on Chester County on 1 and 2 September 2021 and set record flood levels on the Brandywine. A basement that stayed dry that week was a basement with working drainage and a working pump. One that flooded showed where the system was weak.

If your basement took on water then, write down where it appeared. That location is the best clue the contractor can have when designing drainage.

What a good basement quote should include.

  • A description of where the water is entering and what evidence supports that conclusion.
  • The type and length of drainage, the sump pit and pump size, and whether a backup is included.
  • How the discharge line will be routed and where water will end up outside.
  • How walls and floors will be restored after the work.
  • What warranty applies, who honors it and what it requires of you, such as pump maintenance.

Be cautious of any quote written without looking at the basement or without asking where and when water appears.
