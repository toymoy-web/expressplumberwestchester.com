Why Your Drain Keeps Clogging Even After You Snake It.

If you have cleared the same drain more than once this year, the clog is not the problem. It is a symptom, and treating the symptom keeps costing you.

Written by the West Chester Express Plumbing team. Published 1 October 2026. Updated 1 October 2026.

24/7 emergency line.
(610) 672-7601.
Call Now

A clog is a symptom.

When a drain clogs once, the cause is usually a one-time event, such as a washcloth or a hairball. When it clogs again in a few weeks, something is catching debris. That something could be a rough patch on the pipe wall, a root intrusion at a joint, a low spot where water pools, or a habit that keeps feeding the line.

A cable opens a path through the blockage but leaves the pipe much as it found it. If the pipe has a coating or a defect, the clog returns.

Why clogs come back.

How to find the cause.

1

Note where it clogs.

A single fixture points to its branch. Several fixtures point to the main line.

2

Note when it returns.

Weeks suggests wall buildup. Months suggests roots. After storms suggests infiltration.

3

Run a camera.

A camera shows the pipe's condition and ends guessing.

4

Match the fix to the cause.

Jetting for buildup, cutting and repair for roots, replacement for a sag.

What jetting does that a cable cannot.

A cable punches a hole through a clog. Jetting a drain line uses water at high pressure to scour the walls, removing the grease and scale that make the next clog likely. With a camera before and after, you can see the difference.

Jetting is not right for every pipe. A cracked or collapsed section can be damaged by pressure, and that is another reason to look first. Emergency drain cleaning with a cable remains the right tool for a single blockage in a healthy pipe.

Habits that cause repeat clogs.

  • Pouring cooking grease down the sink, where it cools and sticks to the pipe.
  • Flushing wipes labeled flushable, which do not break down like toilet paper.
  • Letting hair wash down the shower drain without a catcher.
  • Running the garbage disposal with fibrous scraps like celery and corn husks.
  • Using chemical drain openers repeatedly, which can damage older pipes.

West Bradford homes and older lines.

Older houses in West Bradford, including those in and around Marshallton, often have a mix of drain materials, cast iron at the main with newer plastic branches. Cast iron develops rough scale inside that collects debris, so repeat clogs in such a house are common. A camera inspection tells you whether jetting will help or whether the line has reached the point where lining or replacement is cheaper than constant cleaning.

What a camera sees in a drain that keeps clogging.

Most repeat clogs look ordinary on camera. The pipe is intact, and a rough, uneven coating of grease and soap runs along the bottom half. In kitchen lines the coating is yellow or white and waxy.

In bathroom lines it is gray and gritty with hair woven through it. The cable that cleared the last clog went straight through the middle of that coating without touching it.

Other pipes show something different. A joint where one section sits slightly lower than the next catches paper every time. A thin hair of root pokes through a gasket and snags everything that passes.

A long, shallow dip holds a few inches of water at all times. Each looks different on screen and calls for a different answer, which is why the camera pays for itself before any cleaning begins.

Treat the habit or treat the pipe.

Change the habit when.

  • The clog is always at the same single fixture.
  • The debris pulled out is hair, grease or wipes.
  • The pipe looks smooth on camera.
  • Clogs began after a change in how the fixture is used.

Fix the pipe when.

  • Several fixtures clog in the same season.
  • The camera shows roots, a sag or an offset.
  • Cleaning gives relief for weeks, not months.
  • The pipe material is old cast iron or clay with rough walls.

Most households need a bit of both. Habits slow the buildup and the pipe decides how fast it narrows.

A sensible order of operations for a problem drain.

1

Clear the immediate clog.

A cable or auger restores flow now. Keep the debris that comes out, since what it is tells you something.

2

Ask for a camera pass.

Cleaning and then scoping the same visit is common. Ask for the footage and the distance to anything unusual.

3

Pick the follow-up.

Jetting for coated walls, root cutting plus repair for intrusion, replacement for a sag. If the camera shows a clean pipe, change the habit and monitor.

4

Set a check date.

Mark a calendar date three to six months out. If the drain is still clear, the fix worked.

What not to expect from drain products.

Enzyme and bacterial products sold for drains can help keep a clean pipe clean, particularly in kitchen lines, but they do not clear a blockage and they do not remove thick buildup. Liquid openers work on small hair and soap clogs, but they sit in a pipe that is already full, and they are harsh on older metal. A plunger, a hand auger and a strainer in every shower are the inexpensive tools worth owning.

If a drain is clogged and the usual tools do not move it, stop adding products. Mixing different cleaners in one line can produce harmful fumes, and the plumber who arrives has to work around the chemicals left in the water.

Costs to weigh against each other.

A single cable clearing costs less than any other fix, which is why it is tempting to keep doing it. Three or four clearings a year add up to more than one jetting and a camera pass, and the pipe is no better afterward. If the line has a defect, the repair eventually costs more than the cleaning would have, plus the cleaning bills you paid in the meantime.

Add the cost of a surprise backup on a weekend night, and the cheaper path is usually to find the cause now.
