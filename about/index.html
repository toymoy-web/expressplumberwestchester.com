What this website is.

West Chester Express Plumbing is an independent advertising and referral service for people in West Chester, Pennsylvania and the townships around it. It is not a plumbing contractor. When you call (610) 672-7601, the call is answered and connected to a local plumbing provider who can take your job.

That means the provider you speak with sets the price, decides availability and holds whatever licenses and registrations their work requires. This site does not perform plumbing work, and it cannot promise an arrival time. What it does is answer the phone at any hour and point you toward a provider who serves your township.

Why the pages are written the way they are.

Most plumbing websites say the same things. These pages try to answer the questions a person actually has while standing in a wet basement or looking at a high water bill. Each service page explains how to tell what is wrong, what the usual fixes are, what changes the price and what to ask before agreeing to work.

Where a local fact matters, the page uses it. Pages on septic work point to the Chester County Health Department, which issues on-lot sewage and well permits. Pages on digging point to Pennsylvania's 811 call requirement.

Pages on water quality use the utility's own annual report. Where a fact could not be confirmed, such as local water hardness, the pages say so instead of guessing.

Sources used on this site.

  • Aqua Pennsylvania's 2025 Water Quality Report for the West Chester Division, for water sources, lead and copper results, fluoride and PFAS sampling.
  • The Chester County Health Department, for on-lot sewage and well permitting and the licensing of haulers.
  • PA One Call (811) and the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, for excavation notice rules.
  • West Chester Borough's published plumbing code on ecode360, for permit and licensing practice, which should always be confirmed with the borough's codes office.
  • Township websites and county planning records, for municipal facts and addresses.
  • Local news reporting and county statements on the September 2021 flooding.

How to check a plumber before you hire one.

Pennsylvania has no statewide plumbing license, so licensing is set locally. West Chester Borough's code requires permits and a license for plumbing work, and the surrounding townships have their own rules. Ask the provider who will pull the permit and whether an inspection is required.

Pennsylvania also requires home improvement contractors above a set annual volume to register with the Attorney General's office, and homeowners can look up a registration online. Ask for a written quote that names the work, the price and any call-out fee before work begins.

How a call works from start to finish.

You dial (610) 672-7601. The call is answered and routed to a plumbing provider who covers your township. You describe the problem, the provider asks a few questions, and you hear a price range or a quote before anything is scheduled for emergency work. For non-emergency work such as a water heater replacement, the provider can arrange a visit to look at the job first.

Two things are worth knowing in advance. The provider who answers may differ from one call to the next, because calls go to whoever is available for your area. And the call may be recorded, as the privacy policy explains.

If a quote sounds wrong, you can decline and call someone else. Nothing about the referral obliges you to hire anyone.

What to do before you hire anyone.

A short checklist covers most of the risk. Confirm the provider's business name and where they are based. Ask who will pull the permit if the job needs one.

Ask for the quote in writing, including any call-out fee. And ask what the warranty covers and for how long. Honest providers answer these without fuss.

For emergencies, the first priority is to stop the damage. Close the main water valve, switch off power or gas to a leaking water heater, and keep out of rooms where water meets electricity. The pages on this site describe those steps for each situation, so you can take them while you wait.
