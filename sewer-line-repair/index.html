Diagnosis.

Why a sewer line needs a camera before a quote.

Two sewer lines can show the same symptom for opposite reasons. One has roots at a single joint and needs a short repair. The other has a long sag and needs replacement. Without a camera, both look like a clog.

A camera inspection records the pipe's inside, shows the distance to the problem, and gives you a recording to compare quotes against. It is the single best way to avoid paying for the wrong repair.

Emergency service.

Emergency sewer line repair in West Chester: when to call tonight.

A collapsed, cracked or root-split sewer line can send waste into the basement or the yard. If sewage is rising in a floor drain or several fixtures are failing together, treat it as an emergency, because it is a health hazard as well as a plumbing fault.

The plumber starts with a camera to see whether the line is blocked, offset or broken, and the repair options are explained with a price before digging or lining begins.

What to do until the emergency plumber arrives.

1

Stop using water.

Do not run sinks, showers, dishwashers or washing machines. Every gallon ends up in the lowest drain.

2

Keep people and pets away.

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children out of the area and do not walk through it barefoot.

3

Locate your cleanout.

If you know where the cleanout cap is, tell the dispatcher. It speeds the first visit.

What causes sewer lines to fail.

Sewer laterals fail from age, ground movement, roots and the pipe's own material. Clay pipe has joints that open as soil shifts. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out and scales shut.

Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe used in some mid-century homes, deforms and collapses. PVC is durable, but can sag if the trench was poorly bedded.

Roots are an effect rather than a cause. They find tiny leaks and open joints, then grow into the pipe because it is full of water and nutrients. Cutting them clears the line, but the joint stays open until it is repaired or lined.

Trenchless or traditional.

Traditional excavation fits when.

  • The pipe has collapsed or has major sags.
  • The line is shallow with many bends.
  • Repairing connections to the main also needs access.

Trenchless fits when.

  • The pipe is structurally sound but cracked or leaking at joints.
  • Landscaping, a driveway or a patio sits over the line.
  • Entry points can be limited to one or two small pits.

Before any digging.

Pennsylvania law requires anyone planning excavation to contact PA One Call by dialing 811 at least three business days beforehand. The call is free for homeowners, and utilities mark their lines so the crew does not hit gas, water or electric. The Public Utility Commission also says contractors should submit their own tickets rather than asking homeowners to do it.

The borough and townships may require permits for sewer lateral work, and the requirement differs by municipality, so ask the plumber who will handle it.

Planning for the repair.

  • Ask the plumber to mark the location on the surface and show you the route on the footage.
  • Identify what sits above the line, such as a driveway, a patio, a tree or a fence.
  • Confirm who will handle the 811 call, any permit and the inspection.
  • Ask how access to the house plumbing will work while the lateral is out of service.
  • Request a written description of the repair method and what restoration is included.

If a camera shows only a minor defect, it is reasonable to monitor it with a follow-up scope in a year or two instead of repairing at once. A good plumber will say so.

What to do before any digging.

Call 811 at least three business days ahead, as Pennsylvania law requires, and keep markings visible. Sewer work follows the plumbing code and the municipality's lateral standards.
