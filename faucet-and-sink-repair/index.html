Diagnosis.

Find the drip before you buy a part.

Faucets drip for four ordinary reasons: a worn washer or cartridge, a pitted valve seat, a cracked O-ring, or mineral scale holding the valve open. Each fix is different, and swapping the wrong part is the most common reason a faucet still drips after a repair.

Where the water appears also matters. A drip from the spout points to the cartridge or seat. Water at the base of the handle points to an O-ring. Water under the cabinet points to the supply line, the shutoff valve or the sink drain, and no faucet part will cure that.

Match the symptom to the repair.

Symptom.Common cause.Repair.
Drip from the spout when off.Worn cartridge or seat.Replace the cartridge and seat.
Water leaking around the handle.Cracked O-ring or loose packing.Replace O-rings and tighten.
Weak flow from one faucet only.Clogged aerator or scale in the cartridge.Clean the aerator, descale or replace the cartridge.
Wet cabinet floor.Supply line, shutoff valve or trap.Replace the leaking part and test under pressure.
Handle stiff or loose.Mineral buildup or worn stem.Clean or replace the stem.

Check these before the plumber arrives.

A few minutes of looking can save a diagnostic trip.

  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If they will not close, mention it when you call, because that changes the plan.
  • Dry the cabinet and the pipes with a towel, then run the faucet for a minute and look for the first bead of water. That bead shows the real leak.
  • Unscrew the aerator at the tip of the spout. If debris falls out, the low flow may be fixed already.
  • Take a phone photo of the faucet and the pipes beneath it. A brand name on the body helps the plumber arrive with the right cartridge.

Repair or swap the faucet.

Repair is the better choice when.

  • The faucet is a standard brand with common parts.
  • The body is not corroded or pitted.
  • You like the finish and fit.

A new faucet is the better choice when.

  • Parts are discontinued or cost close to a new unit.
  • The finish is flaking or the body is corroded.
  • You are changing the sink or countertop anyway.

If you are replacing the faucet, the supply lines and shutoff valves are worth replacing at the same time. They are cheap parts that fail without much warning.

Leak under the sink right now?

Close the shutoff valves and call. A small leak is cheaper to fix than a ruined cabinet.
