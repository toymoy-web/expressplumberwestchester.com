Symptom-first.

My water tastes or smells off. What do I do?

A chlorine taste, cloudy water, visible sediment or a metallic aftertaste each point to a different fix. Taste and odor are usually handled by activated carbon. Sediment is handled by a mechanical filter at the main. Specific health-related contaminants need a system certified to remove them.

That is why a good installer starts with the question "what do you want reduced?" before recommending equipment.

Whole-house or point-of-use.

A whole-house filter treats every tap and appliance. It suits sediment, chlorine taste and scale prevention goals. A point-of-use filter treats only one tap, usually the kitchen, and suits drinking and cooking water where you want the strongest treatment, such as reverse osmosis.

Many homes use both: a sediment and carbon filter at the main, then a smaller system under the kitchen sink. That keeps the expensive membrane or cartridge protected from particles and extends its life.

What West Chester's utility report says.

Aqua Pennsylvania's 2025 Water Quality Report for the West Chester system says most of the water comes from Brandywine Creek and two wells. Tap samples for lead and copper in 2025 showed none above the action level: the 90th percentile for lead was not detected, and copper was 0.04 ppm against an action level of 1.3 ppm.

In voluntary PFAS sampling at entry points, the report lists PFBS at 2.5 to 2.8 parts per trillion and no detections of GenX, PFHxS or PFNA. The water is also fluoridated. These figures describe the water leaving the system, not what flows through older plumbing inside your house, so a point-of-use filter is a personal choice rather than a requirement.

Look for these before you buy.

Certification matters more than the brand.

  • NSF/ANSI 42 for aesthetic effects such as chlorine taste and odor.
  • NSF/ANSI 53 for health-related contaminants such as lead.
  • NSF/ANSI 58 for reverse osmosis systems.
  • A replacement cartridge price and schedule you can accept, because cheap filters with expensive cartridges cost more over time.

Filtration and the rest of the plumbing.

Hard water scale is a separate problem from taste. If you see white scale on fixtures or your water heater is short-lived, look at water softener installation. If a filter housing is leaking, that falls under plumbing repair help.

Maintenance that keeps a filter working.

  • Mark cartridge change dates on the housing or in your phone, since forgotten filters can clog and lower flow.
  • Keep spare cartridges on hand for the most important system in the house.
  • Check the pressure gauge before and after the filter. A big drop means the filter is clogged.
  • Sanitize housings when changing cartridges to avoid biofilm.
  • Test your water yearly if you rely on a filter for a specific concern.

No filter is a substitute for fixing a plumbing problem. If water is discolored right after repairs, flush the lines first and then look at filtration.

What to do before choosing a filter.

Pick a filter certified to the NSF/ANSI standard for the contaminant you care about: 42 for taste and odor, 53 for health-related contaminants and 58 for reverse osmosis. Never rely on a filter alone for water that a utility has told you not to drink.
