Why a toilet runs, leaks or will not flush.

A running toilet usually has a flapper that no longer seals, a fill valve that does not shut off, or a chain that is too tight or too loose. A toilet that rocks or leaks at the base has a failed wax ring or a damaged flange. A toilet that will not flush well can have a partial clog or a rim jet blocked with scale.

A running toilet is a quiet water waster. A leak at the base is a hidden one, because it can soak the subfloor before you see anything.

Emergency service.

Emergency toilet repair in West Chester: when to call tonight.

An overflowing toilet, a base that leaks onto the floor, or a cracked tank pouring water is an emergency. A toilet that only runs on can be handled by closing the small valve behind it and booking a scheduled repair.

If more than one fixture is backing up at the same time, the problem is not the toilet. The main line is likely blocked and the call becomes an emergency drain or sewer call. Say so on the phone.

What to do until the emergency plumber arrives.

1

Close the toilet stop valve.

Turn the small valve behind the toilet clockwise. Lift the tank float to stop the fill if the bowl is rising.

2

Do not flush again.

Each flush adds water. Wait for the plumber if the bowl is near the rim.

3

Dry the floor.

Remove mats and rugs, mop up water and open a window to limit odor and moisture.

Choosing a replacement toilet.

A new toilet is a simple swap if you match three things. The rough-in distance, measured from the wall behind the toilet to the center of the floor drain, is commonly twelve inches. The bowl shape, round or elongated, must fit the space. And the flush rating, shown as gallons per flush, sets water use.

WaterSense-labeled toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less. Comfort-height bowls sit a couple of inches higher than standard, which many people prefer. Dual-flush and pressure-assisted models cost more and behave differently, so decide what matters before shopping.

A running toilet, step by step.

1

Lift the tank lid.

Look at the flapper and the chain. A kinked chain can prop the flapper open.

2

Add dye.

A few drops of food coloring in the tank show a leak into the bowl without flushing.

3

Check the fill valve.

If the water level is above the overflow tube, the fill valve is not shutting off.

4

Replace the worn part.

Flappers and fill valves are inexpensive and swap quickly.

What a running toilet costs.

A running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons of water a day, which shows up on the bill and on the meter. The repair is among the cheapest in plumbing, so waiting is rarely worth it. If the meter keeps moving after you fix the toilet, something else is leaking and leak detection visits is the next step.

What to do before a toilet repair.

Close the valve behind the bowl, mop up quickly and keep children away from overflow water. Toilets and installations follow the flush volume standards set by the EPA WaterSense program and the plumbing code.

A toilet that keeps clogging in a house on septic is a tank question before it is a toilet question, and the East Bradford page explains who permits septic systems in Chester County. Ask whether your house is on public sewer.

Student housing near West Chester University sees toilets overused when the term is busy. A running or clogged toilet in a rental is a call the landlord and the plumber usually sort out together, so have the address and unit ready.

Common toilet repairs and when to replace.

A running toilet is usually a flapper or fill valve. A leak at the base points to a failed wax ring, and a cracked bowl or tank means replacement.

Specialty toilets such as one-piece, bidet or washlet models need parts that standard repairs do not use. Mention the brand so the plumber can plan the visit.
