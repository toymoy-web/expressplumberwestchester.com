Symptoms.

Hum, click, leak or nothing at all.

A disposal that hums but does not spin has a jammed flywheel. A single click usually means the same thing, and a motor that does not respond at all has most likely tripped its red reset button on the underside of the unit or lost power at the wall switch.

Water is a different problem. A leak at the top rim points to the sink flange seal. A leak from the middle seam points to a cracked housing, and a leak at the side outlet points to the drain hose or the dishwasher connection.

Where the leak is tells you the fix.

Leak location.Cause.Typical fix.
Under the sink flange at the top.Dried plumber's putty or a loose mounting ring.Reseat the flange with new putty.
Middle seam of the housing.Corroded or cracked housing.Replace the disposal.
Side drain outlet.Loose or worn gasket on the drain elbow.New gasket, tighten the elbow.
Dishwasher inlet on the side.Loose hose clamp or cracked hose.Replace the hose or clamp.
Bottom of the unit.Failed seal around the motor shaft.Replace the disposal.

Clearing a jam without hurting yourself.

1

Switch it off at the wall.

Unplug the unit or flip the breaker so it cannot start while you work.

2

Look from above with a flashlight.

Use tongs or pliers to lift out visible debris such as a bottle cap, a fruit pit or a piece of glass.

3

Turn the flywheel by hand.

Most units have a hex socket in the bottom. Work a wrench back and forth until the flywheel moves freely.

4

Press reset and test.

Press the red reset on the underside, restore power, and run cold water for a short test. If it trips again, the motor is likely worn.

Repair or replace the disposal.

Repair is reasonable when.

  • The unit only jammed and spins freely again.
  • A hose, gasket or flange seal is the leak.
  • The motor is quiet and the unit is fairly new.

Replace when.

  • The housing leaks from the seam or the bottom.
  • The motor hums, trips and fails repeatedly.
  • The unit is older and rust is visible.

Disposals are sized by horsepower. A half-horsepower unit handles light use, while a three-quarter or one-horsepower unit copes better with a busy household kitchen.

Disposal leaking into the cabinet?

Close the shutoffs under the sink and call the line. Dispatch can start while you dry the cabinet.

What to do before you touch a jammed disposal.

Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit or flip the breaker before any work. Never put fingers into the chamber, and follow the manufacturer's guidelines for the reset button and the hex socket.
