Common problems that need a plumber.

Banging pipes, weak flow, a valve that will not close and a drip at a fitting each have a different cause and a different cure. Water hammer is usually a sign of fast-closing valves and loose pipes. Low pressure at one tap is a local problem, while low pressure everywhere points to the main valve, a pressure-reducing valve or a leak.

A good repair starts by isolating which of those you have.

How a plumber narrows a vague problem.

When a problem is not clear, the plumber works from the water supply outward. Pressure is checked at an outdoor spigot or the water heater. If it is normal, the issue is local to a fixture. If it is low, the plumber checks the main valve, the pressure regulator and the service line.

Noises get the same treatment. A knock that happens when a specific tap is closed points to that tap's valve and the pipe it feeds. A hum that comes and goes may be a pressure regulator. A hiss in a wall can be a small leak, and that deserves attention before the stain appears.

  • Where the water heater's shutoff and power or gas control are.
  • The age and material of the visible supply piping: copper, galvanized steel, PEX or plastic.

Permits and who may do the work.

West Chester Borough's plumbing code requires a permit for plumbing work and a license for the person doing it, and the code issues permits to master plumbers. An owner may pull a permit for work they do personally on their own single-family home. Small repairs like a washer or a shutoff may not trigger one, but replacing or re-routing pipes usually does.

What to do before a repair visit.

Know where the main shutoff is and keep clear of wet outlets. Repairs follow the plumbing code, and valves and fittings should meet the standards printed on them.
