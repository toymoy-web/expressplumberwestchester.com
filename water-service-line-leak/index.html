Water Service Line Leak Repair in West Chester, PA.

The service line is the pipe that carries water from the street into your house. It runs underground, so a leak can go unseen for months.

Symptom-first.

Why is my yard wet when it has not rained?

Water bubbling in the yard, a soft patch in the lawn, a sudden pressure drop, or a meter that keeps moving when nothing is on can all signal a leak in the service line. Because the pipe is buried, the first sign is often the water bill.

The question to settle early is where the leak is. It might be in the part of the line the utility owns, or in the part you do.

The three sections of a service line.

A service line is not one pipe with one owner. Starting at the water main under the street, a tap and a valve connect to a pipe that runs to the curb, where a curb stop valve can shut off water to the house. From the curb stop, the line continues to the meter, which may be in the basement or in a pit near the street.

From the meter, the house side of the line carries water through the foundation to the main shutoff.

Responsibility changes along that path. The utility generally owns the main and the connection, and the property owner generally owns the part from a dividing point to the house. The dividing point differs by utility, so ask yours before assuming.

How a service line leak is repaired.

1

Call 811.

Utilities mark their lines. The call must be placed at least three business days before digging.

2

Locate the leak.

Acoustic equipment and pressure tests narrow the spot.

3

Choose the method.

A short repair, a trenchless replacement or an open-cut replacement are compared.

4

Repair, flush and test.

The line is repaired, flushed, pressure tested and backfilled.

Aqua Pennsylvania and the meter.

Aqua Pennsylvania supplies the West Chester system and lists its customer line as 877-987-2782. Aqua's 2025 report says a service line inventory is available at aquawater.com/leadmap.

If the leak is on the street side of the meter, report it to Aqua. If it is on the house side, the repair is generally the owner's, so a plumber should document where the leak is before work begins.

Protect yourself before any digging.

  • Locate your main shutoff inside the house and your curb stop outside, and check that both turn.
  • Photograph the meter and its reading so the leak test has a before and after.
  • Ask the plumber to confirm in writing where the leak sits relative to the meter.
  • Make sure the 811 call is placed by the contractor, with the dig area marked in white paint or flags.
  • Ask what restoration is included, such as grass seed, gravel, asphalt patching or sidewalk repair.

Old service lines may be lead, galvanized steel, copper or plastic. If yours is being replaced, ask the plumber what the new pipe is and what the old one was.

What to do if you see water in the yard.

Do not dig. Close the main, note the spot and call 811 before any excavation, as the law requires. Water line materials should meet NSF/ANSI 61 standards for drinking water.
