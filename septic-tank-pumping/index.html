Who has septic around West Chester.

West Chester Borough is on a public sanitary sewer. Several of the surrounding townships have areas on public sewer and areas on private on-lot systems. East Goshen Township's own history notes that much of its land was zoned for one house per acre with on-lot sewer and well, and public sewer was later extended into parts of the township.

If you are not sure which you have, check your sewer bill, look for a tank lid in the yard, or ask your township office.

Emergency service.

Emergency septic tank pumping in West Chester: when to call tonight.

A full tank can push sewage back into the lowest drain or surface in the yard. Alarm lights, gurgling in several fixtures and wet ground near the tank are signs to call tonight, not next week.

Say whether the tank was recently pumped, where the lid is, and whether the pump or alarm panel is showing a fault. Heavy use, such as guests or laundry, often tips an overdue tank over.

What to do until the emergency plumber arrives.

Skip laundry and long showers. Do not use the garbage disposal.

2

Keep children and pets away from wet ground near the tank or drainfield.

3

Find the access lid.

Clear snow and soil from the lid if you know the location, and tell the dispatcher.

What actually happens inside a septic tank.

A septic tank is a buried, watertight box. Wastewater enters, solids settle to the bottom as sludge, grease and light material float as scum, and the clearer liquid in the middle flows out to the drainfield. Bacteria in the tank break down part of the solids, but not all of it, which is why tanks need pumping.

If sludge and scum build up until they reach the outlet, solids pass into the drainfield and clog it. A drainfield replacement costs far more than regular pumping, which is why the interval matters.

The county sets the rules.

In Pennsylvania the Chester County Health Department, rather than the township, issues permits for the installation of individual on-lot sewage systems and wells and enforces Act 537 regulations for on-lot systems. Its office is at 601 Westtown Road in West Chester, and permit questions go to 610-344-6225. The department also licenses liquid waste haulers and pump installers in the county.

That matters when you hire anyone for septic work. Ask whether the hauler is licensed through the county, and have the pumping receipt kept with your home records.

How often to pump, and why it varies.

Factor.Effect.Result.
Household size.More people means more solids.More frequent pumping.
Tank size.A larger tank holds more before filling.Longer intervals.
Garbage disposal use.Adds solids to the tank.Pump sooner.
Water use.Heavy use leaves less settling time.More wear on the drainfield.

A common guideline is to have the tank inspected every year or two and pumped every three to five years, adjusted for these factors.

Signs the system needs attention.

  • Slow drains or gurgling throughout the house.
  • Sewage odors near the tank or the drainfield.
  • Wet, spongy or unusually green patches over the drainfield.
  • Sewage backing up into the lowest fixtures.
  • An alarm sounding on a pump chamber.

Related work.

A full backup inside the house is an emergency covered under what to do when sewage comes up. If the problem is a clog in the line to the tank, drain cleaning visits may clear it. A camera can check the line between house and tank through camera inspection of the sewer line.

Habits that protect a septic system.

  • Spread laundry across the week instead of washing many loads in one day.
  • Keep grease, wipes, feminine products and cooking oil out of the drains.
  • Use bleach and harsh cleaners in moderation, since they can harm the bacteria in the tank.
  • Keep vehicles and heavy equipment off the tank and drainfield.
  • Fix dripping fixtures and running toilets, which flood the drainfield over time.
  • Keep records of pumping and inspection dates in one place.

If you are buying a home with septic, ask for the county permit record and the last pumping receipt. Both are part of understanding what you are buying.

What to do if sewage surfaces or backs up.

Keep children and pets away from wet ground over the field and stop using water. Septic safety follows Pennsylvania's on-lot sewage standards, which the Chester County Health Department enforces.
