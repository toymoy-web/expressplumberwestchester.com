Symptom-first.

Why does my tankless heater run lukewarm or shut off?

A tankless unit that cycles between hot and cold, shuts off mid-shower or shows an error code usually has one of three problems. The heat exchanger is scaled, the inlet filter is clogged, or the flow is too low to trigger the burner. Each is fixable without replacing the unit.

That is why a service call starts with the code and the water path, not with a quote for a new heater.

What the common error codes are telling you.

Every manufacturer uses its own codes, so the manual or the label inside the cover is the final word. Still, the families are consistent. Ignition codes mean the burner did not light, often from a gas supply, igniter or flame sensor issue.

Ventilation codes mean the exhaust or combustion air is restricted. Temperature or flow codes point to scale, a closed valve or a low-flow fixture.

Write the code down before power cycling the unit. Resetting clears the display but also clears the clue, and the pattern of repeat codes is often what identifies the cause.

What a descaling visit involves.

1

Shut off power and gas.

The unit is isolated so the service valves can be used safely.

2

A small pump circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger.

3

Run the cycle.

The solution runs for the period the manufacturer specifies, usually around forty-five minutes to an hour.

4

Rinse and reassemble.

Clean water flushes the system, the inlet filter is cleaned, and the unit is tested.

How often to descale.

There is no universal interval. Manufacturers often suggest descaling about once a year in hard-water areas. Aqua Pennsylvania's 2025 West Chester report does not publish a hardness figure, so the most reliable approach is to test your own water and watch for symptoms such as lower flow or noise.

If the unit has run for years without a flush, the first descaling may take longer and is worth doing before it fails.

Everyday habits that help a tankless unit.

  • Open the hot tap fully rather than barely, because very low flow can fail to trigger the burner.
  • Clean the inlet filter screen when you notice flow dropping.
  • Keep the area around the intake and exhaust free of lint, dust and storage.
  • Schedule a flush on a regular cycle, and more often if you see scale on fixtures.
  • Avoid running very low volumes of hot water between uses if you can, to reduce the cold-water sandwich effect.

A tankless unit that is cared for tends to outlast a tank heater by years. One that is neglected can lose efficiency quietly, and the bill is the first place it shows.

What to do if a gas unit shows a venting or ignition code.

Shut off the gas and power if you smell gas, and leave the house if the smell persists. Venting must follow the manufacturer's installation guidelines and the fuel gas code the municipality enforces.

Flushing and descaling a tankless unit.

A tankless heater needs periodic flushing to remove scale from the heat exchanger, and the right interval depends on your water. Error codes, lukewarm water and noisy operation are common signs.

Flushing uses a pump and descaling solution through the service valves. If your unit has no isolation valves, they can be added at the same visit.
