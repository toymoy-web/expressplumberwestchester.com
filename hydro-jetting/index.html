Symptom-first.

Why does my drain clog again after snaking?

A cable opens a channel through a blockage but leaves a coating of grease, soap scum or scale on the walls. That coating narrows the pipe, and debris catches on it again. Within weeks the drain slows, and you are back where you started.

Hydro jetting addresses that coating. A high-pressure water stream scours the pipe walls, restoring the diameter that the pipe had when it was new.

What the nozzle actually does inside the pipe.

A jetting nozzle is a metal head with several small holes. Water at high pressure exits those holes in a pattern that depends on the design. Rear-facing jets pull the nozzle forward through the pipe and push debris back toward the opening.

Side jets wash the walls. A forward jet breaks up the plug ahead.

Because the water does the cutting, jetting leaves the pipe interior smoother than a cable does. A cable can score a pipe if the cutting head is too aggressive, while a jet follows the pipe's shape and removes only what is stuck to it.

How a jetting visit works.

1

Inspect first.

A camera confirms the pipe is intact enough to jet and shows where buildup sits.

2

Choose the nozzle.

Forward-facing nozzles open a clog. Rotating nozzles spray backward to scour walls. Root cutters handle roots.

3

Jet the line.

The hose feeds through a cleanout while water pressure, typically in the low thousands of psi, scours the pipe.

4

Verify.

A second camera pass shows the pipe walls and confirms flow.

Snaking or jetting.

Snaking is enough when.

  • A single clog, such as paper or a toy, is the cause.
  • The pipe walls are otherwise clean.
  • You need the fastest, lowest-cost fix.

Jetting is better when.

  • Grease or soap scale keeps returning.
  • Roots are growing back.
  • The camera shows heavy coating on the walls.

Jetting is not a repair. A cracked or offset pipe may leak water into the soil when jetted, so the camera step matters.

Where jetting fits in West Chester.

Restaurants and commercial kitchens near the borough's downtown often schedule jetting to control grease, and residences with older cast iron lines benefit when scale builds up. If the camera reveals a defect, sewer line repair follows. For basic clogs, start with clearing a stubborn clog.

When a plumber will decline to jet.

  • The camera shows a cracked or collapsed section, where pressurized water could erode the soil outside the pipe.
  • The pipe is old clay with failing joints, where pressure can push roots and soil further in.
  • There is no access point, such as a cleanout, and pulling a toilet would put the hose at risk.
  • The line is connected to a failing septic tank or a field line that cannot take surge flow.

Declining to jet is a good sign, not a bad one. It means the plumber is matching the method to the pipe, which is what keeps a cleaning from turning into a repair.

Jetting for restaurants and larger buildings.

Kitchens put cooking grease through drains every day, and grease cools into a solid layer on the pipe wall. Cabling opens a path, but the layer rebuilds. Jetting on a schedule, matched to the kitchen's use, keeps the pipe walls clean and avoids Saturday-night backups.

Residential owners with a large family or a history of grease clogs can use the same approach on a smaller scale, checking with a camera to decide how often cleaning is actually needed.

What to do before a jetting visit.

Clear the area around the cleanout, keep pets and children away and tell the crew about any known damage. Jetting pressures are chosen according to the pipe material and the camera findings.
