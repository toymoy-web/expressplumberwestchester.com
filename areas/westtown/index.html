Seasonal first.

Winter, storms and the emergency plumbing in Westtown.

Westtown's calls follow the weather. Cold snaps bring frozen lines in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls. Big storms bring sump pump failures and basement water. Hot, dry weeks bring irrigation leaks and running toilets that nobody notices until the bill arrives.

Having the main shutoff location, the sump pump condition and the water heater age in your head before the season changes is the simplest preparation. If a faucet slows to a trickle on a freezing morning, close the main and call before the pipe splits.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

What to do to limit damage in Westtown while a plumber is on the way.

  • Close the main, and in a garage or crawlspace pipe freeze, leave the tap open so melt water can escape.
  • Do not use a torch on a frozen pipe, and shut the main if a split shows when the ice melts.
  • In a large or institutional building, close the zone valve for the affected wing before the main if you know where it is.
  • Photograph the split and the staining before drywall comes down.
  • Call the 24/7 line, say Westtown, and give the road: Wilmington Pike, Chester Road, Street Road or Westtown Road.

A township built around a school and four main roads.

Westtown School opened in May 1799 on 600 acres bought in 1794, and it describes itself as the oldest continuously operating coeducational boarding school in the United States. The township is also crossed by several main roads: Wilmington Pike (U.S. 202 and 322), Chester Road (PA 352), West Chester Pike (PA 3) and Street Road (PA 926).

For plumbing, the useful point is variety. Large institutional buildings, older farmhouses and newer subdivisions all share the township, and each calls for something different. Institutions rely on service for shops, offices and kitchens, older houses on repiping decisions and newer houses on routine repairs.
