The crossroads first.

Where Route 30 meets Route 100, and what sits under the pavement.

West Whiteland began as the western half of Whiteland Township, which was organized around 1704 and divided by court decree on February 26, 1756. The village of Exton grew where the old Lancaster Road crossed the north to south road that is now Route 100.

After the Exton Square Mall opened, the township's population rose from about 7,900 in 1973 to more than 16,000 by 2000. A farmhouse built in 1750 still stands on the mall grounds, so pipes in the township range from colonial stone to subdivisions and big commercial buildings.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

What to do to limit damage in West Whiteland while a plumber is on the way.

  • Close the main valve where the water line enters the house, then open a cold tap on the lowest floor to relieve pressure.
  • In a basement that has taken on stormwater, stay out of the water if the panel or outlets are near it, and call before touching the sump.
  • If a water heater is leaking, switch off its power or gas control and close the cold supply above it.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and photograph the damage before you wipe it up.
  • Call the 24/7 line and name the road, such as Route 100, Route 30 or Pottstown Pike, plus the nearest cross street.

Flash floods, Valley Creek and the township sewer.

On the evening of July 25, 2023, flash flooding in West Whiteland led to 16 water rescues by police and firefighters. Reports from that night describe water going over walls at the Exton train station and onto Route 30 and Route 100, and a nearby restaurant had to close.

The township has since compiled a database of high flood risk areas, and its Stormwater Task Force reviews reported trouble spots. A Valley Creek flood study is also part of the township's work. For a homeowner the practical steps are a working sump pump, a battery backup and knowing the main shutoff. If water has already entered, mitigation after a leak is the next call.

West Whiteland Township operates a public sanitary sewer system and pays for the pumping and treatment of each gallon that enters it. In 2024 the township reported lining about 5,667 linear feet of sewer main as part of a five year plan. That is the township's main, not the pipe from your house to the street.

The lateral that links a house to the main is normally the owner's to fix. If a camera shows the blockage is past your property line, call the township office on Commerce Drive and report it.
