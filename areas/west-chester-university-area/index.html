The area.

Housing near the university.

West Chester University's North Campus and the Chester County government both lie within the borough, according to its Wikipedia entry. The West Chester State College Quadrangle Historic District is also on the National Register of Historic Places.

The borough's planning inventory describes a Southeast planning area near the university and notes the borough's highest count of rental units there. It also records converted single-family houses and rooming houses, mostly in that area. No exact boundary for this part of town is published, so this page does not claim one.

What the planning figures say.

The inventory, built on 1990 census and 1999 county assessment data, put the Southeast area at 792 renter-occupied units, about 67 percent of its occupied homes. It counted about 235 rental rooms in rooming houses across the borough, mostly in the Southeast, and left out roughly 3,000 dormitory units. The borough average was 57 percent renter occupied.

Those numbers are old and describe the borough's planning areas, not any one street. They are included to show that rentals make up a large share of the housing near campus, which is why this page is written for landlords and tenants.

Older and converted buildings can have plumbing added in stages, so an owner who has not seen the pipes in a while may not know what is behind a wall. A plumber can trace the lines when a repair is needed, and general maintenance such as checking shutoffs, water heater age and under-sink leaks is worth doing between tenants.

Common situations near campus.

Shared houses.

A house with several tenants has one set of pipes and one water heater. A running toilet or a slow drain affects everyone, so report problems early.

Landlord and tenant.

Tenants should tell the landlord about a leak right away. Landlords can describe the property and approve the repair.

Turnover season.

Rental properties often change hands in summer. A quick walk through for leaks, running fixtures and slow drains can prevent a surprise.
