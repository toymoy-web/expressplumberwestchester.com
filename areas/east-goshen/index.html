Emergency plumbing along Paoli Pike, West Chester Pike and Route 202.

East Goshen is crossed by U.S. 202 in its northwest tip, PA 3 (West Chester Pike) along its southern portion, and Paoli Pike, Boot Road and North Chester Road through its middle. The first Quaker meeting house in the area was built at the crossroads of Paoli Pike and North Chester Road around 1702, in the village known as Goshenville.

A call from Paoli Pike reaches a different part of the township than one from Boot Road, so name the road you are on and the nearest cross street.

Public sewer and on-lot systems side by side.

East Goshen's own township history notes that most of its land was once zoned for one house per acre with on-lot sewer and well, and that public sewer and water were later extended into parts of the township. The result is a mix. Some streets are on the township's public sewer, with sewer billing handled by the township, and some properties still use septic and a private well.

That mix changes the first question on a drain call. Do you have a septic tank? If so, a backup may mean a full tank, not a clogged lateral, and pumping a septic tank comes before drain cleaning. If you are on public sewer, a camera can check the lateral.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

What to do to limit damage in East Goshen while a plumber is on the way.

  • Turn off the main valve and drain the lines from a low tap before the water reaches the floor drain.
  • Gas water heaters on this side of the township share a flue with the furnace in many houses, so shut off the heater's gas control and leave the vent alone.
  • If you have a septic tank, stop all water use after a backup rather than flushing to test.
  • Take photographs of the failed fitting and the damage before cleanup.
  • Call the 24/7 line, say East Goshen, and give the road: Paoli Pike, West Chester Pike, Boot Road or North Chester Road.
