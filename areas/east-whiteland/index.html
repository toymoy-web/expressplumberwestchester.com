Valley floor and ridges.

Emergency plumbing across the Great Valley, from Frazer to the Malvern addresses.

East Whiteland lies in the Great Valley, which runs west to east toward the Schuylkill River. A ridge of hills marks the northern and southern edges of the township, and Valley Creek and Little Valley Creek cross the uneven valley floor between them. Mailing addresses here read Malvern, Frazer or, in a small area, Exton.

Whiteland Township was founded in 1704 and divided into East and West Whiteland in 1765. The Lapp Log House, St. Peter's Church in the Great Valley, the Spring Mill Complex and the White Horse Tavern are all on the National Register of Historic Places. Old buildings and new ones share the same roads, so the plumbing behind each front door can be very different.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

What to do to limit damage in East Whiteland while a plumber is on the way.

  • Shut the main water valve, then open the lowest tap in the house so the pipes drain instead of pushing water through the split.
  • In a basement that sits low in the valley, look at the sump pit before you step into water, and stay away from the electric panel if the floor is wet.
  • In a restaurant, shop or office suite, close the branch valve for the affected kitchen or restroom and move stock off the floor.
  • If you smell gas near a water heater or boiler, leave the building and call 911 before anything else.
  • Call the 24/7 line, say East Whiteland, and name the road: Lancaster Avenue, Conestoga Road, Morehall Road or Sproul Road.

What the township's growth means for the pipes.

East Whiteland counted 1,740 residents in 1950 and 5,078 in 1960, then 7,242 in 1970, according to the census figures on its Wikipedia page. The township reached 13,917 people in 2020, up 30.7 percent from 2010. That means two very different generations of plumbing live side by side, and a repair call in one is not the same as a call in the other.

Houses from the first boom are now old enough that galvanized or early copper lines, original shutoff valves and aging tanks fail without warning, and a failed tank is often a water heater replacement call. Newer homes tend to fail at fittings, hoses and appliance connections instead. Near Valley Creek and Little Valley Creek, groundwater is the other thing to watch, so keep the sump pump tested before a wet season.
