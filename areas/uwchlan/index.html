A Welsh name and a crossroads village.

Uwchlan began as Welsh farmland and grew into a suburb.

Most of the early settlers here came from Wales, and Uwchlan is a Welsh name often translated as "above the parish." The township dates to 1712. Its best-known village, Lionville, was first called Welsh Pool, then Red Lion, and got its present name when the post office would not accept a name already used by too many Pennsylvania towns.

The population was 995 in 1960, 5,473 in 1970 and 19,161 in 2020, so most of what you see today was built within living memory. Marchwood, a housing development begun in the early 1960s south of the Route 100 and 113 crossing, sits in the same township as the newer Eagleview. Houses from different decades mean water heaters, supply lines and sump pumps of very different ages.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

What to do to limit damage in Uwchlan while a plumber is on the way.

  • Close the main valve where the water line enters the house, usually in a basement or utility room, then open the lowest cold tap to drain the pipes.
  • If a drain is backing up in a house on public sewer, stop running water and do not flush, because every flush adds to what is coming up the floor drain.
  • A leaking water heater needs its power or gas control turned off and its cold supply closed.
  • Keep out of a wet basement if the floor is flooded near the electrical panel, and photograph the damage from the stairs for your insurer.
  • Call the 24/7 line and give Uwchlan, Lionville or Exton plus your street and the nearest cross street, such as North Ship Road or Uwchlan Avenue.

Sewer mains, pump stations and water main work.

Uwchlan Township's sewer division maintains the sewer mains and pump stations, and the township shares in operating the Downingtown and Eagleview treatment plants. Its website lists a separate after-hours line for sewer emergencies and asks residents to report clogged or overflowing sanitary sewer lines. If neighbors on your street are backing up at the same time, the township should hear about it as well as the plumber.

If only your own house is affected, the blockage is usually in your own line.
