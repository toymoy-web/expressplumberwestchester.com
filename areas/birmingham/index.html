Battlefield township first.

An old Quaker township that kept its open land.

Birmingham is the oldest township in Chester County. William Brinton secured two patents from William Penn near Dilworthtown in 1684 and named the place after his home city in England, and the Brandywine became its western boundary.

The entire township sits inside the Brandywine Battlefield National Historic Landmark, which is a large part of why farmland and stone farmhouses survived here. Radley Run, begun in 1965, was the first suburban development, so pipe age and layout can differ a lot between neighboring addresses.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

What to do to limit damage in Birmingham while a plumber is on the way.

  • Close the main water valve, which in an older stone house may sit in a cellar corner, and open the lowest cold tap to drain the lines.
  • If a toilet or floor drain is backing up and the house has a septic tank, stop running water instead of flushing to test the system.
  • Switch off the water heater at its breaker or gas control and close its cold supply if the tank is the source.
  • Photograph the failed pipe and the wet areas for your insurer, and note the time you shut the valve.
  • Call the 24/7 line, say Birmingham, and name the nearest road such as Birmingham Road, Street Road, Wilmington Pike or Brintons Bridge Road.

Septic tanks carry most of Birmingham.

The township's own septic page states that the majority of properties are served by on-lot septic systems, and that upkeep is the homeowner's responsibility. It advises pumping the tank every two to three years and notes that a garbage disposal adds solids the tank cannot break down well.

That makes a backup here a different problem from one on a city sewer, because the fix may be a tank, a baffle or the absorption field rather than the street main. The township stormwater office takes calls about failing septic systems, and neighbors with the same backup are worth mentioning to dispatch.
